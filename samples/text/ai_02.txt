\nIn the late 1800s, there were two main types of electrical systems being developed: direct current (DC), which was promoted by Thomas Edison, and alternating current (AC), which was promoted by Nikola Tesla.\n\nDC power is a type of electrical power that flows in one direction, from a positive terminal to a negative terminal. It is used in many electronic devices, such as batteries and cellphones.\n\nAC power is a type of electrical power that alternates its direction of flow, typically at a frequency of 50 or 60 Hz (hertz). It is used to power most homes and businesses.\n\nThe debate between DC and AC power centered on which type of power was better for transmitting electricity over long distances. Edison believed that DC power was better because it was easier to distribute and use. However, Tesla argued that AC power was superior because it could be transmitted over much longer distances and at higher voltages, making it more efficient.\n\nUltimately, AC power won out and is now the most widely used type of electrical power. However, DC power is still used in many applications, such as in batteries and electronic devices. So, both types of power are still in use today.
